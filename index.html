Houhai and the Drum Tower

Sun 20 Sep · Evening · Landmark, reframed

The Drum and Bell Towers kept time for the imperial city; the lakes behind them were where ordinary Beijingers swam, skated and drank. This is still one of the last hutong districts with residents in it, though the pressure to hollow it out is constant. Use tonight to get your bearings; everything on the Beijing list radiates from here.

Your hotel is 5 minutes from everything here.

Zhang Mama 张妈妈特色川味馆

Sun 20 Sep · Dinner · Local food

Sichuan food came to Beijing with migrants from the southwest, and Zhang Mama is the version of it that Beijing students and young office workers name when you ask where to eat well for the price of a sandwich. One cramped room, shared tables, no English, and the numbing heat is not dialled down for anyone. Order the twice-cooked pork, a dry-pot, and the cold noodles. It is a good first meal because it is a walk from your hotel and it sets the register for the rest of the trip.

Jiaodaokou branch on Jiaodaokou Nan Dajie. Around ¥50 a head, expect a queue, and check it is still trading before you walk over: the branches move.

Tiananmen Square

Mon 21 Sep · Morning · People's history

Every popular movement of the 20th century met here. May Fourth 1919: students marched against the Versailles handover of German concessions to Japan, and against their own government's weakness. April 1976: the mass mourning for Zhou Enlai became the first open defiance of the Cultural Revolution leadership; the crowd was cleared by force on 5 April. April to June 1989: students, then workers (the Beijing Workers' Autonomous Federation set up its tents on the north-west corner), held the square for seven weeks. None of this is marked. The one thing to read is the ten marble reliefs at the base of the Monument to the People's Heroes: the state's own selection, but the subjects are the Opium War burning, the Taiping, the 1911 uprising, May Fourth, the May Thirtieth strike of 1925, and the Nanchang mutiny. The Party tells the story of popular revolt right up to the day it took power.

Advance reservation with passport via WeChat mini-program is required. Book before you fly.

Chang'an Avenue west to Muxidi

Mon 21 Sep · Late morning · People's history

On the night of 3–4 June 1989 the 38th Army entered the city from the west along Chang'an Avenue. The heaviest killing was at Muxidi, where residents of the apartment blocks (many of them retired officials) threw up barricades; the death toll there dwarfed the square itself. Muxidi is now a metro station and a row of ministries. There is nothing to see, and that is the point of walking it.

Stay on the north pavement past Xidan; Muxidi is 3.5 km. Or ride the metro two stops and walk back.

Xidan Democracy Wall (site)

Mon 21 Sep · Late morning · People's history

From November 1978 a 200-metre brick wall along Xidan became the place where people posted big-character posters calling for democracy, human rights and an accounting for the Cultural Revolution. Wei Jingsheng's essay 'The Fifth Modernization' went up here. Deng tolerated it while it helped him against the old guard, then closed it in December 1979 and jailed Wei for fifteen years. The wall is gone; the site is a mall.

On your route to Muxidi; a two-minute stop.

Forbidden City

Mon 21 Sep · Afternoon · Landmark, reframed

The imperial palace complex, 1420 to 1912. The people's-history note is the exits: in 1900 the Boxers and then the eight foreign armies came through these gates; in 1924 the warlord Feng Yuxiang finally evicted the last emperor. It's the group's landmark; treat it as the walk to Jingshan.

Jingshan Park

Mon 21 Sep · Sunset · Landmark, reframed

The artificial hill built from the palace moat's spoil. Locals come here to sing in choirs, dance and practise instruments in the evening; the last Ming emperor hanged himself on the slope in 1644 as a peasant army entered the city. Best view of the Forbidden City for no money.

Free-ish; 10 minutes to the top.

Siji Minfu 四季民福 (Peking duck)

Mon 21 Sep · Dinner · Local food

Two ways to roast a duck in this city. Bianyifang, which claims 1416, seals the bird in a closed oven heated by the walls; Quanjude, founded in 1864, hangs it over an open fruitwood fire, and that is the version that became famous. Quanjude was nationalised after 1949, is now a state-owned chain, and today mostly sells the name to coach parties at three times the price of a better bird. Siji Minfu is where Beijingers went instead: a local chain doing a properly rendered duck, carved at the table, with a queue of families and office workers rather than tour groups. The Nanchizi branch looks out over the moat and the south-east corner tower of the palace. If the group wants one expensive night in Beijing, 1949 Duck de Chine in Sanlitun is the good version of that — but you are paying for the courtyard and the room, not for better duck.

Nanchizi Dajie branch, on the palace moat. Take a ticket and wait; no reservations at peak. Order the duck, the mustard-root salad and the dried-tofu strips.

Great Wall at Mutianyu

Tue 22 Sep · Full day · Landmark, reframed

The Wall is the one landmark where the popular story is older than the official one. It was built and rebuilt by conscript labour, convicts and soldiers; the folk legend of Meng Jiangnü, the widow who walked to the Wall to find her husband, learned he'd been buried inside it, and wept until a section collapsed, has been sung for two thousand years as the peasant's verdict on imperial glory. Mutianyu is less crowded than Badaling and the woods around it are the Wall's best setting.

Hire a driver for the group (about 1h30 each way). Leave by 07:30.

Wudaokou 五道口 and the university district

Tue 22 Sep · Late afternoon · People's history

Haidian is the student quarter: Peking University, Tsinghua and Beijing Language and Culture University within a few kilometres of each other. The 1989 movement started here, not on the square. Hu Yaobang died on 15 April 1989, and within a day the first big-character posters went up on the noticeboard at Peking University known as the Triangle; students argued there for weeks, then walked the twelve kilometres down to Tiananmen. The Triangle was demolished in 2007. Beijing Language and Culture University, founded in the 1960s to teach Chinese to foreign students, is where May studied. Terracotta Army

Qin Shihuang unified China by conquest, standardised the written language and buried himself with an army of clay. The state's version stops there. Chaotianmen and the bangbang porters 棒棒军

Fri 2 Oct · Late afternoon · People's history

Chaotianmen is where the Yangtze and Jialing meet and where the city's porters have carried cargo up the hill for centuries. In the 1990s hundreds of thousands of migrant men from the countryside worked as 'bangbang', carrying anything for anyone on a bamboo pole; they were the labour that built the modern city and had no place in it. A few hundred are left, mostly old. The documentary 'Last of the Bangbang' (2014) is about them. The site is now under a luxury complex; the river steps below are still theirs.

The Raffles City complex sits on top now; go down to the river steps.

Nanbin Road: the treaty-port consulates

Fri 2 Oct · Evening · People's history

Britain forced Chongqing open as a treaty port in 1891, and the French, Americans and Japanese followed; the south bank filled with consulates, missions, and the Imperial Maritime Customs, run by foreigners collecting China's own tariffs. The buildings are preserved along Nanbin Road and in the Ciyun Temple area. A quiet walk with a river view before dinner, and the last stop before your late flight.

Across the river from the hotel. Cross by the Dongshuimen bridge on foot.
