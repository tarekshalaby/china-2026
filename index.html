Tiananmen Square

Wed 23 Sep · Morning · People's history

Every popular movement of the 20th century met here. May Fourth 1919: students marched against the Versailles handover of German concessions to Japan, and against their own government's weakness. Dahua 1935 大华1935

Chongqing Industrial Museum 重庆工业博物馆

Fri 2 Oct · Morning · People's history

In 1938, as Wuhan fell, the Hanyang steelworks were dismantled and shipped up the Yangtze to Chongqing under bombing, and rebuilt at Dadukou to make the steel for the war. Chongqing Iron and Steel employed generations of workers until the plant relocated in 2011; the halls now house the museum, with the 1905 rolling mill still in place. The exhibits lean on the workers' own accounts, which is rare.

Dadukou, 40 min by taxi or metro line 2. Free.

Lunch in Dadukou 大渡口

Fri 2 Oct · Lunch · Local food

Dadukou exists because the steelworks was moved here under bombing in 1938, and the district was built around it for the workforce: dormitories, schools, clinics, canteens, noodle shops. The plant relocated in 2011 and took the jobs with it, but the eating economy is still there and still aimed at people on shifts and pensions — one-dish rice plates, xiaomian, a plate of greens. Eating here is the second half of the museum visit, and it costs almost nothing.

Look for a shop with a queue of people in work clothes. Xiaomian, rice plates, stir-fries, nothing over ¥15.

Three Gorges Museum and People's Great Hall

Fri 2 Oct · Afternoon · Landmark, reframed

The Great Hall is Chongqing's grand 1954 auditorium, built in the first years after liberation. The museum opposite has the city's history; skip the Three Gorges dam propaganda and go to the 'Wartime Capital' gallery about the bombing, and the gallery on the dam's displaced 1.3 million people, which is franker than you'd expect.

Free; the Hall is a 1950s Soviet-Chinese hybrid. Then walk to Liziba.

Chaotianmen and the bangbang porters 棒棒军

Fri 2 Oct · Late afternoon · People's history

Chaotianmen is where the Yangtze and Jialing meet and where the city's porters have carried cargo up the hill for centuries. In the 1990s hundreds of thousands of migrant men from the countryside worked as 'bangbang', carrying anything for anyone on a bamboo pole; they were the labour that built the modern city and had no place in it. A few hundred are left, mostly old. The documentary 'Last of the Bangbang' (2014) is about them. The site is now under a luxury complex; the river steps below are still theirs.

The Raffles City complex sits on top now; go down to the river steps.

Nanbin Road: the treaty-port consulates

Fri 2 Oct · Evening · People's history

Britain forced Chongqing open as a treaty port in 1891, and the French, Americans and Japanese followed; the south bank filled with consulates, missions, and the Imperial Maritime Customs, run by foreigners collecting China's own tariffs. The buildings are preserved along Nanbin Road and in the Ciyun Temple area. A quiet walk with a river view before dinner, and the last stop before your late flight.

Across the river from the hotel. Cross by the Dongshuimen bridge on foot, and walk back over it for the cave hotpot.

Cave hotpot 洞子火锅

Fri 2 Oct · Dinner · Local food

Chongqing hotpot began on the docks: porters and boatmen cooked cheap offal in a fiery broth of beef tallow, chilli and Sichuan pepper. After the war, dozens of the city's air-raid shelters became restaurants, and 'cave hotpot' is now the way locals eat it: a long stone tunnel, sweat, and the loudest room you'll be in all trip. The last dinner of the trip, eaten in a tunnel, which is the right place to end it.

Dongting Hotpot 洞亭火锅 near the hotel is one; any 洞子 restaurant works. Order 特辣. Walk back over the Dongshuimen bridge from Nanbin Road; you need to be at the hotel by 22:30.
