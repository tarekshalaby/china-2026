Panjiayuan flea market 潘家园旧货市场

Mon 21 Sep · Morning · People's history

The largest secondhand market in China, and the main place the material of the Cultural Revolution still changes hands: badges, Little Red Books, posters, porcelain Maos, and — the part worth your time — the paper. Family letters, diaries, work permits, self-criticisms and photograph albums, cleared out of flats when an old person dies and sold by weight. Fan Jianchuan built much of the Red Era collection you will see in Chengdu out of markets like this one. Most of the 'antiques' are reproductions and everyone knows it; the paper is usually real and costs a few yuan. What you are looking at is what happened to the physical record of a mass political movement once nobody official wanted to keep it.

Weekdays 08:30–18:00, permanent stalls only. The real market is the weekend ground sellers from 04:30, and your only weekend morning is arrival day.

Wangfujing 王府井 and St Joseph's Church

Mon 21 Sep · Late afternoon · Landmark, reframed

By your standards there is nothing here: this is the pedestrianised centre of consumer Beijing, malls and flagship stores, and until it was cleared away a manufactured 'snack street' selling scorpions on sticks to people who were never going to eat them. What earns the walk is two-thirds of the way up on the east side. St Joseph's Church, the Dongtang, was founded by Jesuits in 1655 on land granted by the Shunzhi emperor. It burned in 1900, when the Boxers moved through Beijing attacking foreign missions and, far more often, Chinese converts, and it was rebuilt in 1904 out of the reparations the eight allied powers extracted from the Qing after the siege — a building that is itself a receipt for an indemnity. The square in front of it is where Beijing teenagers skateboard in the evening, which is the best thing about the street. fRUITYSPACE is fifteen minutes north; treat this as the walk to the gig.

Wudaokou 五道口 and the university district

Tue 22 Sep · Late afternoon · People's history

Haidian is the student quarter: Peking University, Tsinghua and Beijing Language and Culture University within a few kilometres of each other. The 1989 movement started here, not on the square. Hu Yaobang died on 15 April 1989, and within a day the first big-character posters went up on the noticeboard at Peking University known as the Triangle; students argued there for weeks, then walked the twelve kilometres down to Tiananmen. The Triangle was demolished in 2007. Beijing Language and Culture University, founded in the 1960s to teach Chinese to foreign students, is where May studied. Wudaokou itself was the city's student nightlife district — D-22, the bar that launched much of Beijing's indie rock scene, ran here from 2006 until it closed in 2012 — and it is still cheap Korean food and student bars between the new malls.

Luosifen 螺蛳粉

Tue 22 Sep · Dinner · Local food

Luosifen comes from Liuzhou in Guangxi: rice noodles in a broth boiled long and hard from river snails, with sour pickled bamboo shoot that produces a smell people compare to blocked drains and then queue for three times a week. It was a night-market dish until around 2014, when vacuum-packed instant versions turned it into an industry. The Liuzhou government pushed it hard as a poverty-reduction programme, and it now employs tens of thousands there and sells billions of yuan a year, mostly in packets. That is the state's own telling of it — a rust-belt car-factory city lifted out of poverty by a snack — and it is partly true. Eating the fresh version in a student neighbourhood is as close as Beijing gets to where it started.

Student district, so the shops are cheap and everywhere around the station. Not Beijing food — this is Guangxi, and it went national.

Tiananmen Square

Wed 23 Sep · Morning · People's history

Every popular movement of the 20th century met here. May Fourth 1919: students marched against the Versailles handover of German concessions to Japan, and against their own government's weakness. April 1976: the mass mourning for Zhou Enlai became the first open defiance of the Cultural Revolution leadership; the crowd was cleared by force on 5 April. April to June 1989: students, then workers (the Beijing Workers' Autonomous Federation set up its tents on the north-west corner), held the square for seven weeks. None of this is marked. The one thing to read is the ten marble reliefs at the base of the Monument to the People's Heroes: the state's own selection, but the subjects are the Opium War burning, the Taiping, the 1911 uprising, May Fourth, the May Thirtieth strike of 1925, and the Nanchang mutiny. The Party tells the story of popular revolt right up to the day it took power.

Advance reservation with passport via WeChat mini-program is required. Book before you fly.

Xidan Democracy Wall (site)

Wed 23 Sep · Late morning · People's history

From November 1978 a 200-metre brick wall along Xidan became the place where people posted big-character posters calling for democracy, human rights and an accounting for the Cultural Revolution. Siji Minfu 四季民福 (Peking duck)

Wed 23 Sep · Dinner · Local food

Two ways to roast a duck in this city. Bianyifang, which claims 1416, seals the bird in a closed oven heated by the walls; Quanjude, founded in 1864, hangs it over an open fruitwood fire, and that is the version that became famous. Terracotta Army

Fri 25 Sep · Full day · Landmark, reframed

Qin Shihuang unified China by conquest, standardised the written language and buried himself with an army of clay. The state's version stops there. The people's version: 700,000 conscripts and convicts built the mausoleum, many of them buried around it in mass graves the archaeologists have found. Three years after the emperor died, two conscript labourers, Chen Sheng and Wu Guang, late for a work detail and facing execution, launched China's first recorded peasant uprising with the line 'Are kings and nobles born to it?' The dynasty fell within four years. And in 1974 the farmer Yang Zhifa dug a well, found the first head, and was paid ten yuan; he spent his later years signing books at the gift shop.

About 1h by car or the tourist bus from the station. Hire a guide who will talk about the workers.

Huaqing Palace: the Five-Room Hall 华清宫 · 五间厅

Fri 25 Sep · Late afternoon · People's history

Before dawn on 12 December 1936, Zhang Xueliang's Manchurian troops surrounded this compound. Chiang Kai-shek went out of the bedroom window of the Five-Room Hall in his nightclothes and was found a few hours later hiding behind a boulder up the slope of Mount Li. He was held for a fortnight and released on Christmas Day, having agreed to stop the war against the Communists and turn on the Japanese occupying the north-east. The pressure came from below: Zhang's soldiers had lost their homes to Japan in 1931 and were refusing to spend the war killing other Chinese. The Incident forced the united front and changed the shape of the war. Zhang spent the next fifty years under house arrest. Yang Hucheng, who did it with him, was murdered along with his family in a Chongqing prison in 1949 — you will be standing in that prison in nine days. The bullet marks are on the outer wall of the bedroom, the window is where it was, and the boulder has a pavilion over it. The rest of the site is Tang dynasty bathing pools and a Yang Guifei romance; walk through that to get to the Hall.

14 km from the tomb, 20 minutes' drive — do it on the way back. ¥120, open to 18:30. Ask for 五间厅 and the pavilion on the hillside.

Foot massage 足疗

Fri 25 Sep · Evening · Landmark, reframed

Massage in China is not a spa treatment, it is an ordinary evening that costs about the price of two rounds of beer, and after a day at the tomb your legs will make the argument for you. Zuliao is the foot session with a herb soak first; tuina is the deep joint-and-muscle version, and it hurts.

Eighth Route Army Xi'an Office 八路军西安办事处

Sat 26 Sep · Morning · People's history

Once the Xi'an Incident had forced the united front, the Communists were able to run an open liaison office in the city, and they ran it here from 1937 to 1946. Officially it handled telegrams and supplies for the Eighth Route Army. In practice it was the gate to Yan'an: students, writers, teachers and factory workers arriving from the occupied east came into this courtyard to be interviewed, issued a letter and a bedroll, and pointed at the four-hundred-kilometre road north. Estimates of how many went through run into the tens of thousands across those nine years. Zhou Enlai worked out of these rooms. It is furnished as it was — the bunks, the telegraph desk, the bicycles in the yard — and it is the one site in Xi'an that is about ordinary people deciding to go, rather than about generals deciding for them.

Qixian Zhuang, north-east corner inside the wall. Free with passport, about an hour.

Dahua 1935 大华1935

Sat 26 Sep · Afternoon · People's history

Dahua Cotton Mill opened in 1935 and was the first large modern factory in the north-west. Its workers struck that same year against the government's refusal to fight the Japanese advance, and it became the earliest factory in the Xi'an area to have a Communist Party organisation operating inside it. When Japan bombed the city the mill was a target: on 11 October 1939 more than thirty bombs killed around forty people on the site, and a raid that December killed thirty-two more. It passed from private to state ownership in 1951, ran for decades as a key enterprise, and was facing demolition by 2008 before the site was turned into Dahua 1935 — a cultural district with the machinery, the sawtooth roofs and an industrial museum kept inside it, plus a theatre and cafés. This is the labour history you came for, and unlike the mills out at Textile City it is a ten-minute taxi rather than a forty-minute one.

Across the river from the hotel. Cross by the Dongshuimen bridge on foot.

Dinner on Nanbin Road 南滨路

Fri 2 Oct · Dinner · Local food

The restaurant strip on the south bank runs below the old treaty-port consulates, and the view is the entire peninsula rising out of the water in lights. It is the one straightforwardly scenic meal on this list and after two weeks it has been earned: a last hotpot or a table of river fish, eaten outside, watching the cable car cross and the container barges push upstream. The 01:50 flight will feel like somebody else's problem right up until it isn't.

Ask for a river-facing table. You need to be back at the hotel by 22:30 for the airport.
